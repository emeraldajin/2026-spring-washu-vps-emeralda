Reading

My website is a shifting house next to a river of knowledge. What could yours be?

(By Laurel Schwulst)

Review

    • I liked how Schwulst first started out with a simple definition of a website to convey the literal understanding of it, before
      expanding into the metaphorical complexities of a website. I like the emphasis on how the web is meant to serve people and its
      users, rather than becoming an overloading amalgamation of information. When Schwulst stated that websites are rooms because
      finite, and with a specific purpose, I immediately thought of the constrasting platforms such as Instagram reels that sends
      you on a endless and meaningless spiral of videos meant to preoccupy your attention as long as possible, to your detriment
      and their profit. It's surprisingly to see that this issue was already prominent in 2018, before the release of reels.
      My website would likely be a garden, tended to here and there, but mostly left on its own to grow and maybe overrun the
      backyard or die of some unprecendented cause. I'm not the most talented at growing things. I'd like to think of websites
      as leaving my mark, like sprinkling wildflower seeds on the grass just because I like how they look.
      Websites are forms of expression. If someone makes a website, they can try their best to express what you want them to convey,
      but it's ultimately their work, not yours even if it was meant for you. Ownership rights aside, creating a website means carving
      out a space for yourself, and if someone makes one for you then you're residing in a space carved out by another.
