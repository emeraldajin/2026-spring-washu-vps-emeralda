Reading

“The Coming Age of Calm Technology” (2024)

(By Mark Weiser and John Seely Brown)

Review

Even though the text described as mainframe as "many people share a computer" and ubiquitous computing as "many computers share each of us," I still find the seemingly opposite trends very similar. In my understanding, the main differentiation lies in who benefits from the relationship in terms of information (many people using a computer for information vs an AI system that uses many people for information). Technology often works two ways, and even a Mainframe shared computer can be Ubiquitous if the information is collected by the computer for its own purposes. And even then, Ubiquitous technology benefits from us but also gives us something in return (e.g., better trained AI = better performance). The lines feel blurry to me.

As for the second half of the article regarding "calm" technology, I find the periphery explanation fascinating. Consuming isolated information feels more overwhelming than consuming information surrounded by other information in the periphery because of the contrast periphery information provides and the feeling of space created by divions between central and periphery (e.g., an open, empty room feels smaller than two rooms that combine to be the same size as the empty room).
