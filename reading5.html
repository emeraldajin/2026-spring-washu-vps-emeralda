Reading

“Eno” (2024)

(By Gary Hustwit)

Review

Hustwit's documentary reminded me of Avant Garde films I watched in my Intro to Film class the fall of freshman year. I like his style of chaos and creating projects just out of feelings and emotions. I find his emphasis on the beauty of being a small part of something big (e.g., concerts, the planet, etc.) interesting because it finds comfort in the syncrhony and masses instead of reaching for individuality, especially considering that's what the modern world values. Going off of that point, I also liked what Hustwit described about art as a way of keeping people in sync with one another and cooperation between people, as a solution to a modern world where we are "using things that are a product of other people's brains." What differentiates his "generative" works from the AI works that plague the internet is that his works are in sync with one other as a big creation rather than a mismatch and mesh of everyone's works into something unrecognizable where there is no longer synchrony and cooperation but a blended mass labeled as one individual's creation with the label of ownership slapped on over other people's works. For a work to stand in synchrony with others, the generative work should still be able to portray components that make up the entire creation like individual notes of a music piece rather than all the sounds enmeshed in one.
